What you post on Facebook, Instagram and TikTok can reach far more people than you meant, including strangers and scammers who use personal details to sound convincing. This guide sets who sees what. Do the two **Essential** items in each app you use. Meta is renaming **Accounts Center** to **Meta Account**, so you may see either name. The steps follow Meta's and TikTok's help pages for Android; on iPhone the **Menu** button may sit in a different corner.

## Protect your login first {#login-protection .essential}

Privacy settings don't help if someone else can sign in to your account. Turn on two-step verification in each app and keep the recovery codes somewhere safe.

### Facebook and Instagram

1. Follow [Turn on two-step verification](/guides/accounts/#two-step): it covers both apps and explains which method to choose.
2. Check where you are signed in: see [Sign out of devices you don't recognise](/guides/accounts/#devices).

### TikTok

1. Tap **Profile**, then the menu ☰, then **Settings and privacy → Security & permissions → 2-step verification**.
2. Choose at least two methods, an authenticator app among them if you can, and tap **Turn on**.
3. In **Security & permissions → Manage devices**, delete any device you don't recognise, then change your password.

> [!note]
> If you lose your authenticator app and don't have its recovery code, TikTok says getting back in can take up to 30 days. Save the recovery code when you set it up.

## Choose who sees your posts {#audience .essential}

On Facebook, the audience you set in the settings applies to every new post; reels and stories have their own audience setting. On Instagram and TikTok, a private account means you approve who follows you and sees your posts. Whoever can see a post can still take a screenshot and pass it on.

### Facebook

1. Tap **Menu → Settings & privacy → Settings**.
2. Under **Audience and visibility**, tap **Posts** and set who can see your future posts to **Friends**.
3. When you write a post, the audience button under your name changes it for that post only.

### Instagram

1. Go to your profile and tap **Menu** at the top right.
2. Under **Who can see your content**, tap **Account privacy**, turn on **Private account** and tap **Switch to private**.
3. Business accounts can't be made private.

### TikTok

1. Tap **Profile**, then ☰, then **Settings and privacy → Privacy**, and turn on **Private account**.
2. When you post, tap **Settings** at the top of the preview screen to choose who can see that post.

> [!note]
> Some things stay public even on a private account: your name, username and profile photo on all three apps. On Facebook, also your cover photo and your comments on public posts; on Instagram, your bio and the public accounts you follow and that follow you. Posts and comments in a public Facebook group are public too.

## Hide your phone number, email and birthday {#profile-info .recommended}

Your phone number, email and birthday help scammers sound convincing and help strangers find you elsewhere. Show them only to yourself or your friends. Facebook shows your friends list to everyone unless you change it.

### Facebook

1. Go to your profile, tap **Edit profile**, scroll to **Contact Info** and tap **Edit**.
2. For your phone number, tap **Manage contact info** and choose a smaller audience, such as **Only me**. For an email address, tap **Manage contact info**, tap your profile name, then **Change visibility on Facebook**.
3. Under **Personal details**, tap **Edit** next to your birthday and choose who can see the date and the year.
4. In **Settings → Audience and visibility → How people find and contact you**, set **Who can see your friends list?** to **Friends** or **Only me**. A friend whose own list is public can still show that you are friends.

## Stop being found by your phone number or email {#find-by-contact .recommended}

Apps suggest your profile to people who have your number or email in their contacts. That is how a stranger who knows your number can find your profile. Turning this off reduces suggestions but doesn't end them: apps also suggest people based on mutual friends and other activity.

### Facebook

1. Tap **Menu → Settings & privacy → Settings**, then **How people find and contact you** under **Audience and visibility**.
2. Set **People with your email address** and **People with your phone number** to **Friends** or **No one**. Facebook's default is **Possible Connections**.

### Instagram

1. Instagram has no setting that stops it suggesting you to people who have your number or email saved. These steps only stop it using your own address book.
2. Tap **Menu → Meta Account → Your Information and Permissions → Upload Contacts**, turn off **Connect Contacts** and tap **Disconnect**. Do it on every device where you use Instagram.
3. This doesn't delete contacts you uploaded before. To delete them, open Instagram in a web browser and go to **Edit profile → See more in Meta Account → Your information and permissions → Manage Contacts → Delete all**. Deleting can take up to 90 days.
4. In the same browser, on **Edit profile**, turn off **Show account suggestions on profiles** and click **Submit**. This setting is not in the app.

### TikTok

1. Tap **Settings and privacy → Privacy → Suggest your account to others** and turn off **Contacts**, **Facebook friends** and **People who open or send links to you**.
2. In **Privacy → Sync contacts and Facebook friends**, turn off both, then tap **Remove previously synced contacts**.

## Review tags before they appear {#tagging .recommended}

Other people can tag you in photos and posts, which shows you to their audience. Review settings decide what appears on your profile; they don't stop people tagging you. A tagged post you hide from your profile is still visible to its audience in other places, such as Feed and Search.

### Facebook

1. Tap **Menu → Settings & privacy → Settings**, then **Profile and tagging** under **Audience and visibility**.
2. Turn on **Review posts you're tagged in before the post appears on your profile?**
3. Turn on **Review tags people add to your posts before the tags appear on Facebook?**

### Instagram

1. Go to your profile and tap **Menu**.
2. Under **How others can interact with you**, tap **Tags and mentions**, turn on **Manually approve tags** and tap **Turn on**.

### TikTok

1. Tap **Settings and privacy → Privacy → Mentions and tags → Mentions**.
2. Choose **People you follow**, **Friends** or **No one**.

## Don't share where you are {#location .recommended}

A place in a post or a live map can show where you live, work or are right now. Friends can give your location away too, by tagging you or posting a photo of you; ask them not to. TikTok still estimates your rough location from your SIM card and internet connection, even with location access turned off.

### Facebook

1. Limit Facebook's access to your location in your phone's settings: see [Check what your apps can access](/guides/phone/#app-permissions).
2. When you post, don't add a place.

### Instagram

1. Tap **Messages**, then **Map** at the top, then **Settings** at the top right, choose not to share your location with anyone, and tap **Share Location** to save. It is off unless you turned it on. This doesn't stop Instagram reading your phone's location: limit that in your phone's settings, as for Facebook.
2. Don't add a location to posts and stories you don't want tied to a place.

### TikTok

1. Tap **Settings and privacy → Privacy → Location Services**, if you see it, and choose not to give TikTok your location.
2. In the same menu, tap **Delete certain location data**. TikTok may still keep a rough location, such as your city.

## Limit who sees your old posts {#old-posts .advanced}

Years of old public posts can be read by anyone and pieced together. You can narrow them in one go on Facebook; on Instagram and TikTok, you go post by post or in batches.

### Facebook

1. Tap **Menu → Settings & privacy → Settings**, then **Posts** under **Audience and visibility**.
2. Tap **View** next to **Limit who can see past posts**, then **Limit past posts**, and confirm.
3. Your old public posts become visible to **Friends** only. You can't undo this in one step. Posts with a custom audience and posts others tagged you in are not affected. Anyone you tagged in a post, and their friends, can still see it.

### Instagram

1. To hide one post, open it, tap **Options** at the top right and choose **Archive**. Only you can see archived posts, and you can restore them.
2. To archive many at once, tap **Menu → Your activity**, then **Posts** under **Content you shared**, tap **Select**, choose the posts and tap **Archive**.

### TikTok

1. Open a post on your profile, tap **More options** at the side, then **Privacy settings** (you may need to swipe left).
2. Choose **Friends** or **Only you**. TikTok's help page describes no way to change many posts at once.

## Limit how ads follow you {#apps-and-ads .advanced}

Meta and TikTok choose ads from what you do in their apps, and also from what other companies tell them about you. You can reduce the second part; you can't turn ads off.

### Facebook and Instagram

1. In Facebook, tap **Menu → Settings & privacy → Settings → Meta Account → Your information and permissions → Activity from other businesses**. In Instagram, tap **Menu → Meta Account** and go on from **Your information and permissions**. Choose not to see ads based on this activity. It applies to both apps. Companies may still send Meta your activity, and ads can still come from customer lists that businesses upload.
2. In **Meta Account → Ad preferences**, look through **Customize ads** and **Manage info**.

### TikTok

1. Tap **Settings and privacy → Ads** and turn off ads based on data from advertising partners.
2. TikTok still shows you ads based on what you do on TikTok.
